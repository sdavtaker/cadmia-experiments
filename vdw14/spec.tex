\section*{IA-DEVS Formalism}

IA-DEVS (Interval-Approximated DEVS) is a computable approximation of UA-DEVS
in which every quantity --- state, time, input, output --- is replaced by a
closed interval.  An IA-DEVS atomic model is a tuple

\[
  M = \langle S, T, X, Y,\;
      \tilde{\delta}_{\text{int}},\;
      \tilde{\delta}_{\text{ext}},\;
      \tilde{\lambda},\;
      \tilde{ta} \rangle
\]

where $S$, $T$, $X$, $Y$ are base types and the functions operate on their
interval counterparts $\tilde{S} = \mathcal{I}(S)$, $\tilde{T} =
\mathcal{I}(T)$, etc.  All arithmetic is \emph{interval arithmetic}:
operations produce the smallest interval guaranteed to contain every possible
result over the input intervals.

CadmIA represents $\tilde{T}$ as \texttt{interval<decimal<N>>}, a closed
interval over a fixed-point rational type with compile-time scale factor
$10^{-N}$.

\section*{Canonical Model and IA-DEVS Adaptation}

The tick-counter coupled model has $D = \{G_{1/10},\, G_1,\, K\}$ with
\[
  \text{IC} = \{(G_{1/10} \to K.\texttt{input}),\; (G_1 \to K.\texttt{input})\}
  \qquad
  \text{EOC} = \{(K.\texttt{output} \to C.\texttt{out})\}
\]

\subsection*{Portless Coupling}

CadmIA (like CDBoost) does not yet have a typed port system.
Both generators route to $K$'s single \texttt{input\_t}.  The message value
distinguishes the signal:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
  \toprule
  Value & Semantics \\
  \midrule
  $\tilde{v}$ with $0 \notin \tilde{v}$ & add the interval to the accumulator \\
  $\tilde{0} = [0, 0]$                   & output current total, reset to $[0,0]$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection*{Interval Time}

Generator periods are \emph{degenerate} (point) intervals:
\[
  \tilde{p}_{G_{1/10}} = [1/10,\; 1/10],\qquad
  \tilde{p}_{G_1}      = [1,\; 1]
\]

Because no uncertainty is present in the period, all interval arithmetic
reduces to scalar arithmetic.  The scheduled time of the $k$-th $G_{1/10}$
event is the degenerate interval $[k/10,\; k/10]$, computed exactly by
fixed-point addition with no accumulation error.

\subsection*{Counter Transitions (interval)}

Let $\tilde{n} \in \mathcal{I}(\mathbb{N})$ be the accumulated count interval.

\begin{align*}
  \tilde{ta}(\tilde{n},\text{idle})  &= [\infty, \infty] \\
  \tilde{ta}(\tilde{n},\text{ready}) &= [0, 0] \\
  \tilde{\delta}^K_{\text{ext}}\!\left((\tilde{n},\text{idle}),\, \tilde{e},\, \tilde{x}\right)
    &= \begin{cases}
         (\tilde{n} + \tilde{x},\; \text{idle})  & 0 \notin \tilde{x} \\
         (\tilde{n},\;             \text{ready}) & \tilde{x} = [0,0]
       \end{cases} \\
  \tilde{\delta}^K_{\text{int}}\!\left((\tilde{n},\text{ready})\right)
    &= ([0,0],\; \text{idle}) \\
  \tilde{\lambda}(\tilde{n},\text{ready})
    &= \tilde{n}
\end{align*}

\section*{Expected Observations}

With degenerate period intervals, all internal times are exact:
\[
  \tilde{t}_{\text{next}}^{G_{1/10}} = [k/10, k/10], \quad
  \tilde{t}_{\text{next}}^{G_1} = [k, k]
\]
At each integer second $[k,k]$ both generators are simultaneously scheduled;
interval overlap is $[k,k] \cap [k,k] \neq \emptyset$, so simultaneity is
detected.  After ten add steps:
\[
  \tilde{n} = 10 \cdot [1, 1] = [10, 10]
\]
$K$ always outputs the degenerate interval $[10,\, 10]$.

This contrasts with the float case: IEEE~754 accumulation error widens the
effective uncertainty of $t^{G_{1/10}}_k$ until it crosses the $G_1$ boundary,
producing $[9,\, 11]$-range outputs.  IA-DEVS with exact period intervals is
provably immune to this class of causality error.

\section*{Implementation Status}

\textbf{Pending.}  CadmIA does not yet have a coupled-model coordinator, port
system, or runner.  This experiment cannot be executed until those components
